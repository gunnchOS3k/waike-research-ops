# Week 6: Keyboard fluency — gross speed, net speed, accuracy

DCO-3606 is a NO_AI week because it measures your own hands. Job applications and online forms reward people who can type steadily, so the library runs a five-minute practice passage. Typing speed is counted in words per minute, where a 'word' is defined as five characters, spaces included, so long and short words count fairly. Typing 1,250 characters in five minutes is 250 'words' over five minutes, a gross speed of 50 WPM. Mistakes left uncorrected are subtracted per minute: fifteen errors over five minutes costs three, so net speed is 47 WPM. Accuracy compares correct characters with all characters, 1,235 out of 1,250, or 98.8 percent. Most learners improve fastest by slowing down until accuracy stays near ninety-eight percent, then letting speed return, because unlearning a mistyped pattern takes longer than learning it right. Sit square to the keyboard, keep wrists level, and rest the index fingers on the small bumps on F and J. Record the three numbers each week rather than chasing a single best run.

## Worked example

1,250 characters in 5 minutes with 15 uncorrected errors → gross 50 WPM, net 47 WPM, accuracy 98.8%
