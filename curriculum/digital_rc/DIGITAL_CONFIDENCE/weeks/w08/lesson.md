# Week 8: Backups — three copies, two kinds of storage, one away from home

DCO-3808 begins with tears: a laptop died and took two years of family photos with it. A simple rule, called 3-2-1, prevents most of these stories. Keep three copies of anything you would miss, the original plus two backups. Store them on at least two different kinds of storage, so one failure type, such as a batch of bad drives, cannot take everything. Keep one copy somewhere else, away from the house, so a fire, flood, or theft leaves something behind. The library's example plan has the photos on the laptop's SSD, a copy on a USB hard drive at home, and a copy in a cloud folder; that is three copies, three kinds of storage, and one offsite. There is a fourth rule people forget: a backup you have never restored is a hope, not a plan. Once a month, pick a random file and open it from each backup. Two copies on the same USB stick are one copy with extra steps, and a sync folder that mirrors deletions instantly is convenient but is not a backup on its own.

## Worked example

laptop SSD + home USB HDD + cloud folder (offsite) → 3 copies, 3 media, 1 offsite; restore tested → plan_ok
