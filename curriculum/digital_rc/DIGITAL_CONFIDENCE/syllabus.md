# Digital Confidence to Computer Operator — Library Lab

## Who this is for
DIGITAL_CONFIDENCE COURSE_DIGITAL_RC: first-computer confidence for adult and teen learners at a library lab — paths and folders, storage units, password strength, link reading, spreadsheet formulas, keyboard fluency, attachments, 3-2-1 backups, and readable screens. Practice data only. Distinct from GENERAL_IT, which trains support specialists. Not student/teacher E6.

## Tracks and academy
- Tracks: DIGITAL_CONFIDENCE
- Academy: ACADEMY_IT

## Duration
Ten library sessions at 4–6 hours each, paced for adults returning to computers. Weeks 2 (storage units) and 6 (typing) are NO_AI.

## Weekly map
- Week 01: Folders and paths — knowing where a file really lives
- Week 02: Storage units — why a 500 GB drive shows 465 GiB
- Week 03: Passwords and passphrases — strength is length times choice
- Week 04: Reading a link — the real address is at the right end
- Week 05: Spreadsheet formulas — let the sheet do the arithmetic
- Week 06: Keyboard fluency — gross speed, net speed, accuracy
- Week 07: Attachments — why a 19 MB file will not fit under 25 MB
- Week 08: Backups — three copies, two kinds of storage, one away from home
- Week 09: Readable screens — measuring colour contrast
- Week 10: Personal digital safety plan — the page you keep

## Assessments
Library Lab grading: weekly checks on file paths, storage units, passphrase entropy, link reading, spreadsheet formulas, typing speed, attachment limits, 3-2-1 backups, colour contrast, and a personal safety plan; a 20-item midterm on everyday computer arithmetic; a 24-item final on safe operator habits; ten recomputing practice labs; a portfolio a patron could follow.

## Claim boundary
NIST SP 800-63B, W3C WCAG 2.2, and CISA Secure Our World are PUBLIC_REFERENCE_ONLY. Practice files and made-up accounts only; no patron data. Not a digital-literacy certificate. COURSE_DIGITAL_RC only, not REAL_STUDENT_E6 or REAL_TEACHER_E6.

## Kinesthetic hook
Ten Library Lab help-desk cards: find the file → read the drive label → judge a password → read a link → build a budget sheet → measure typing → fit an attachment → plan backups → check screen contrast → write a safety plan.
