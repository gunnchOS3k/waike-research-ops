# Week 4: Ethics ladder — observation before inference

The Harbor Desk ethics ladder is observation → inference → need → action, with fabricated_impact forced false. Ticket PD-2409: three patrons report missing USB files after Friday close. Observation names the reports. Inference may say cleanup script. Need asks for cleanup schedule confirmation. Action pauses wipe and posts notice. Claiming 'this harmed 10,000 residents' without measurement is fabricated_impact and fails.

NO_AI week for the ladder write-up: learners must separate what they saw from what they guess without a model filling gaps. AI may be used later to critique structure, but this week's submission is human-authored ladder JSON.

Consensus Ladder is the lab itself. Confusing observation with inference is the most common Harbor Desk failure mode and it poisons meeting minutes next week. Harbor Desk Voice insists on numbered ticket IDs in journals so a Saturday volunteer can continue without a hallway interrogation. Write the next action as a verb phrase a stranger can execute. Keep patron faces, passwords, and invented harm counts out of the packet. If two tools disagree, write what you saw, then what you guessed, then the missing fact before changing shared systems. Professional development here is measured by reproducible desk artifacts, not by slogans about culture.
